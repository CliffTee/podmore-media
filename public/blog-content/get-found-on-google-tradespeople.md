## Why Google Business Profile Matters for Local Trades

Here's something most tradespeople don't realise: for local searches, your Google Business Profile (GBP) is often the first thing potential customers see.

When someone searches "plumber near me" or "electrician in Watford," Google shows a map with local businesses first. Those listings come from Google Business Profiles — and they appear before regular website results.

Your GBP is free. It takes about 30 minutes to set up properly. And a complete, active profile can help you appear in those local map results.

**What a complete GBP includes:**
- Accurate business name, address, and phone number
- Your service area (the towns and postcodes you cover)
- Your opening hours
- A short, clear description of what you do
- Photos of your work, your van, and yourself
- Regular posts (even one a week helps)

Many tradespeople set up a GBP and then forget about it. That's a missed opportunity. An active, complete profile signals to Google that you're a real, operating business — which can improve your visibility in local searches over time.

## Three Things You Can Do This Week

You don't need a complex marketing strategy. You need three simple actions done consistently.

### 1. Check and Update Your Google Business Profile Photos

Photos are one of the most overlooked parts of a GBP. Google favours profiles with recent, relevant images.

Take 10 minutes to:
- Add a clear photo of yourself (people buy from people) — a simple headshot in your work clothes is fine
- Upload 3–5 photos of recent work — before and after shots work well, or photos of you mid-job
- Take a photo of your branded van — this builds recognition and trust
- Snap a picture of your team if you have one

**What works:** Clear, well-lit photos taken on your phone. A customer kitchen you've just finished. A fuse box you've rewired. Your van parked outside a job. These show real work in real settings.

**What to avoid:** Stock photos, blurry images, or photos with other people's branding visible.

These don't need to be professional shots. Authentic, well-lit phone photos work fine. The key is showing real work in real settings.

### 2. Ask Satisfied Customers for Reviews

Reviews help build trust and can influence your visibility in local search. Businesses with regular, positive reviews tend to perform better.

After you finish a job and the customer is happy, send a quick message:

> "Hi [Name], glad we got that sorted for you. If you have a moment, would you mind leaving a quick review on Google? It really helps other people find us. Here's the link: [Google review link]"

Most satisfied customers will do this if you ask. The trick is asking when the work is fresh and they're pleased with the result.

**How often to ask:** Aim for one new review every week or two if you're doing regular work. Don't ask the same customer multiple times — that's pushy. Focus on new jobs. Over six months, this builds into a solid collection that shows you're active and trusted.

Consistency matters more than volume. Five reviews spread over two months looks better than ten reviews all from one week.

### 3. Post Once a Week on Your GBP

Google Business Profile posts are short updates that appear on your listing for about a week. They show Google (and potential customers) that your business is active.

**What a simple GBP post looks like:**

*Example 1 — Completed job:*
> "New bathroom installation finished in Bromley today. Customer wanted a modern walk-in shower — happy with how it turned out."

*Example 2 — Seasonal tip:*
> "Cold snap coming this weekend. Worth checking your outside taps and pipes. If you're unsure, give us a call."

*Example 3 — Introduction:*
> "Meet Sarah, our apprentice electrician. She's been with us six months and already handling her own call-outs."

You don't need to write essays. One photo + two or three sentences = a post. Do this once a week and over time you'll build more visibility than many competitors who never post at all.

**Why consistency matters:** One post won't change anything. But posting every week for six months signals to Google that you're an active business. It also gives potential customers a sense of what you're like to work with. Think of it as showing your work diary — little glimpses of what you do and how you do it.

## What to Expect (And What Not to Expect)

Let's be honest about what marketing can and cannot do.

**Marketing can:**
- Make you easier to find when people search for your services
- Help you look more professional and trustworthy online
- Keep you visible during quieter periods
- Support a steady flow of enquiries over time

**Marketing cannot:**
- Guarantee a specific number of leads or jobs
- Replace the quality of your work
- Work overnight (it builds gradually over months)
- Fix a business that has fundamental problems

The tradespeople who benefit most from marketing are already good at their work. They just need more people to know they exist.
