# How Tradespeople Can Ask Customers for Google Reviews Without Sounding Pushy

*Published: 27 June 2026 | Podmore Media*

---

You finish the job, the customer is pleased and everything has gone smoothly.

It should be the ideal time to ask for a Google review. Instead, you pack away your tools, send the invoice and tell yourself you will ask later.

Then another job comes in and you forget.

This is common among tradespeople and local service businesses. Asking for a review can feel awkward, especially when you do not want a satisfied customer to feel pressured.

A review request does not need to sound pushy. A short, polite message sent at the right time can make it easy for genuine customers to share their experience.

## Why Google reviews matter to local businesses

Before choosing a plumber, electrician, builder or other local service provider, people often look for signs that the business is reliable and professional.

Reviews can help potential customers understand what previous customers valued. They might mention punctuality, workmanship, tidiness, communication or how a problem was handled.

Reviews can also strengthen the overall appearance of your Google Business Profile. However, they are only one part of it. Your contact details, services, photographs, opening hours and other business information also need to be accurate.

There is no guaranteed number of reviews that will produce more enquiries. Reviews are simply one useful way to show potential customers what dealing with your business may be like.

## Are businesses allowed to ask for Google reviews?

Yes. Google provides businesses with a direct review link and QR code that can be shared with customers.

The review should reflect the customer’s genuine experience, without the business trying to control the rating or wording.

Ask for an **honest review**, rather than asking for five stars.

Do not:
* offer a discount, gift or free service in return;
* tell the customer what rating to leave;
* write the review for them;
* insist that they mention particular services, keywords or locations;
* ask only the customers you expect to leave positive feedback;
* pressure someone into reviewing you while you are still in their home.

It is reasonable to check that a customer is satisfied and deal with any problem before sending a review request. However, this should be part of good customer service, not a system for allowing only happy customers to leave reviews.

## Ask at the right time

The best time to ask is usually soon after the job has been completed and the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to assess the work.

For a small repair, that might be later the same day or the following morning.

For a larger job, it may be better to wait until the customer has properly seen or used the finished work.

For example:
* a decorator might wait until the room has dried and been put back together;
* a heating engineer might follow up after the customer has used the system;
* a landscaper might wait until the customer has seen the completed garden in daylight;
* a cleaner may ask after the customer has returned to the property.

The aim is to ask while the experience is fresh, but not before the customer can reasonably judge the result.

Waiting several weeks makes it more likely that the request will be forgotten or ignored.

## Make leaving a review easy

Do not make the customer search for your business and work out where to leave the review.

Use the direct review link available through your Google Business Profile. Save it somewhere you can reach quickly, such as:
* your phone notes;
* a saved text or WhatsApp reply;
* an email template;
* your invoicing system;
* a customer follow-up spreadsheet;
* your customer-management software.

Test the link before using it. Make sure it opens the correct Business Profile, particularly if another business has a similar name.

The fewer steps the customer has to take, the easier it is for someone who intends to leave a review to complete it.

## Use a short, personal message

You do not need a complicated review request.

Here is a straightforward example:

> Hi [Name], thanks again for choosing us to help with [brief job description]. If you have a moment, would you mind leaving an honest Google review about your experience? It helps other local customers when they are deciding who to contact. Here is the link: [review link]. Thanks, [Your name/business name]

This message is clear, polite and easy to act on.

Adapt it to match the way you normally communicate. A brief WhatsApp message may suit your business better than a formal email.

The request should sound like it came from you, not from the marketing department of a large company.

## Let customers use their own words

It can be tempting to ask customers to mention the service and location you want your business associated with.

For example:

> Please mention that we installed your boiler in Croydon.

Avoid doing this.

Let customers describe the experience in their own words. Those natural details are often more useful to future customers anyway.

One person may mention that you arrived when promised. Another may appreciate that you explained the available options clearly. Someone else may comment on how tidy you left the property.

Together, those comments give people a more believable picture of the business than a collection of carefully directed reviews.

## Add the request to your job-completion routine

The main reason businesses fail to ask for reviews is often not embarrassment. It is the lack of a repeatable process.

Build the request into the way you finish each job.

A simple routine could be:

1. Complete the agreed work.
2. Check that the customer has no immediate questions or concerns.
3. Deal properly with any issue raised.
4. Send the invoice or completion message.
5. Send the review request at an appropriate time.
6. Record that the request was sent.
7. Send one polite reminder if necessary.
8. Thank the customer if they leave a review.

This can be managed with a spreadsheet, diary reminder or customer-management system. You do not need expensive software.

A simple process followed consistently is more useful than a complicated system that gets ignored.

## Should you send a reminder?

People are busy. A customer may intend to leave a review and then become distracted.

One polite reminder after several days is reasonable.

For example:

> Hi [Name], just a quick reminder in case my earlier message was missed. There is no pressure, but an honest Google review would be greatly appreciated if you have a moment: [review link]. Thanks again.

After that, leave it.

Repeated reminders can turn a reasonable request into an annoyance. A customer may be perfectly happy with the work but still prefer not to leave a public review.

Respect that decision.

## Respond to the reviews you receive

When someone takes the time to leave a review, reply where practical.

A short, personal response shows that the feedback has been read and appreciated.

Instead of using the same generic reply every time, acknowledge something relevant:

> Thanks, Sarah. I’m pleased we could find the fault and get the heating working again before the weekend. Thank you for taking the time to leave a review.

Keep the response professional and avoid turning it into an advert.

Be careful not to reveal private information. Do not mention a customer’s address, personal circumstances, payment history or details of a dispute in a public reply.

If a review is critical, avoid starting an argument. Acknowledge the concern calmly and invite the customer to contact you directly where appropriate.

A professional response can reassure future readers, even when the original review is negative.

## Avoid review shortcuts

A smaller number of genuine reviews is better than an impressive-looking rating created through questionable methods.

Avoid:
* buying reviews;
* asking people who were not genuine customers;
* reviewing your own business through another account;
* offering incentives;
* asking customers for a particular star rating;
* repeatedly chasing people who have not responded;
* attempting to prevent dissatisfied customers from giving feedback.

The purpose of a review system is not to create the appearance of perfection.

It is to make it easy for real customers to share real experiences.

## Start with your next completed job

You do not need to launch a major review campaign.

Start with three simple actions:

1. Find your direct Google review link.
2. Save the message template somewhere accessible.
3. Send it after your next suitable completed job.

Once the request becomes part of your normal follow-up process, you will no longer need to remember it from scratch each time.

No review process can guarantee more enquiries. However, asking consistently and professionally can help your Google Business Profile reflect more of the good work your business is already completing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I ask customers to leave a five-star Google review?

Ask for an honest review rather than a particular star rating. The customer should be free to describe their experience and select the rating they believe is appropriate.

### Can I offer a discount in return for a review?

No. Do not offer money, discounts, gifts, free products or free services in return for someone posting, changing or removing a review.

### When should a tradesperson ask for a review?

Ask after the work is complete and the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to assess it. For many smaller jobs, later the same day or the following day will be suitable.

### How many reminders should I send?

One polite reminder is normally enough. Do not repeatedly chase customers who have chosen not to respond.

### Should I respond to every Google review?

Replying where practical shows that you read and value customer feedback. Keep responses brief, relevant and professional, and avoid sharing private customer information.
