You set up your Google Business Profile, verified it and added the basic details. But months later, it still doesn't seem to be helping your business.

That does not necessarily mean Google Business Profile is ineffective. The problem may be incomplete information, the wrong business category, outdated details, weak photos or a lack of recent activity.

This guide walks through the most common issues to check and shows you how to improve them.

## The Wrong Primary Category

Your primary category tells Google what you actually do. Get this wrong and you won't show up for the searches that matter.

We see this often: a heating engineer has listed themselves as "Plumber" because that's close enough. Or a bathroom fitter has chosen "Construction company" because it was the first option that seemed relevant.

The problem? Google uses your primary category to decide which searches to show you for. If someone searches "boiler repair near me" and you're listed as a generic plumber, you might not appear. If they search "bathroom installation" and you're down as a construction company, you could miss the listing entirely.

**What to check:**
- Log into your Google Business Profile
- Go to the "Info" section
- Look at your primary category
- Ask yourself: if a customer searched for this exact term, would they find what they're looking for?

**How to fix it:**
Choose the closest specific category Google offers for the work you actually want to be found for. A bathroom fitter, heating engineer, electrician, or plumber should avoid broad categories that do not clearly describe the main service they provide.

Google provides a dropdown as you type. Choose the option that most accurately describes what you actually do — not what sounds most impressive.

## Your Services Aren't Listed Properly

Once you've set your primary category, you need to list your specific services. This is where many profiles fall down.

Google lets you add services under your main category. These help you appear for more specific searches. But many tradespeople either skip this step entirely or add vague, unhelpful entries.

**Common mistakes:**
- Leaving the services section blank
- Adding generic entries like "Plumbing services" or "Electrical work"
- Listing services you don't actually offer just to appear in more searches
- Not updating services when your business changes

**How to fix it:**
Add 5–10 specific services you actually provide. Think about what customers actually type into Google.

Instead of "Plumbing services," try:
- Emergency leak repair
- Boiler installation
- Bathroom fitting
- Tap and toilet repairs
- Radiator replacement

Instead of "Electrical work," try:
- Fuse box upgrades
- EICR certificates
- Outdoor lighting installation
- Socket and switch replacement
- Electric vehicle charging points

Be honest. Don't list services you can't deliver just to get calls — you'll waste everyone's time.

## Your Service Area Is Unclear or Wrong

This is a subtle but important one. Google needs to know where you work. If your service area is wrong, you won't [show up for customers who are actually nearby](/blog/get-found-on-google-tradespeople).

**Common problems:**
- You've only listed your business address, so Google thinks you only serve that exact location
- Your service area includes towns you don't actually cover
- You've left the service area blank and Google has made assumptions
- Your service area is too narrow or too broad

**How to check:**
In your Google Business Profile, look at your service area settings. Are the towns and postcodes listed the ones you actually work in?

If you travel to customers, make sure you've added all the areas you cover. If you only work locally, be specific about that boundary.

**How to fix it:**
Add the specific towns, cities, or postcodes you serve. Be realistic — don't claim a 50-mile radius if you only work within 10 miles. But don't sell yourself short either. If you regularly work in Croydon, Bromley, and Dartford, make sure all three are listed.

Think about where your best customers come from. Those are the areas to prioritise.

## Outdated Details: Phone Numbers, Hours, and Descriptions

An outdated profile looks dormant. Worse, it frustrates potential customers who can't reach you or turn up when you're closed.

**What to check:**
- Is your phone number current and correct?
- Are your opening hours accurate — including seasonal changes?
- Does your business description still reflect what you actually do?
- Is your website link working?

**Common issues we see:**
- Old mobile numbers that no longer work
- Hours that say "Open" when you're actually closed
- Descriptions copied from an old website that no longer applies
- Links to websites that have changed or disappeared

**How to fix it:**
Go through every field in your profile and verify it. Call your own number to check it works. Check your hours against your actual schedule. Update your description to match your current services.

This takes 10 minutes. But it makes the difference between a profile that works and one that actively puts customers off.

## Weak or Missing Photos

Photos aren't optional extras. They're one of the first things potential customers look at — and one of the signals Google uses to assess your profile.

A profile with no photos looks suspicious. A profile with old, blurry, or obviously stock photos looks unprofessional.

**What to check:**
- Do you have at least 5–10 photos on your profile?
- Are any of them more than a year old?
- Are they clear, well-lit, and relevant?
- Do they show your actual work, or generic images?

**How to fix it:**
Spend 20 minutes capturing new photos on your phone:
- One clear headshot of yourself (customers want to see who they're hiring)
- 3–4 photos of recent jobs (before and after shots work well)
- One photo of your branded van (builds recognition)
- One photo of your tools or workspace (shows professionalism)

Upload them with simple descriptions: "New boiler installation in Croydon" or "Complete rewire in Bromley."

These don't need to be professional shots. Authentic, well-lit phone photos are better than polished stock images.

## Lack of Recent Reviews

Reviews matter. They build trust with potential customers and signal to Google that your business is active and valued.

But many tradespeople have profiles with three reviews from two years ago. That looks dormant. It suggests you might not be trading any more.

**What to check:**
- How many reviews do you have?
- When was your most recent review?
- Are you actively asking satisfied customers to leave feedback?

**How to fix it:**
Start requesting reviews consistently. After you finish a job and the customer is happy, send a brief message:

> "Hi [Name], glad we got that sorted. If you have a moment, a quick review on Google would really help. Here's the link: [your review link]"

Do this when the work is fresh and the customer is pleased. Don't ask the same person multiple times. Focus on new jobs.

Over a few months, this builds into a solid collection that shows you're active and trusted.

## No Regular Posts or Updates

A Google Business Profile isn't a one-time setup. It's an ongoing presence. Profiles that are never updated can start to look neglected, especially beside competitors who post regularly, reply to reviews, and keep their information current.

Regular activity gives both Google and potential customers more current information about your business. Posts, review replies, and updated details all help your profile look alive and trustworthy.

**What to check:**
- When did you last post on your GBP?
- Have you posted at all since setting up the profile?
- Do you respond to reviews and questions?

**How to fix it:**
Post once a week. It doesn't need to be elaborate.

Share a photo of a job you completed. Mention a seasonal tip. Introduce a team member. Highlight a service you offer.

Examples:
- "Just finished a full bathroom refit in Bromley — customer wanted a walk-in shower and modern tiling."
- "Cold weather coming. Worth checking your outside taps and pipe insulation this weekend."
- "Meet Tom, our apprentice plumber. Six months in and already handling emergency call-outs."

One photo and two sentences is enough. The key is consistency, not length.

## Quick Diagnostic Checklist

If you only have 30 minutes, work through this list:

1. **Check your primary category** (5 mins) — Is it specific and accurate?

2. **Review your services list** (5 mins) — Are your actual services listed clearly?

3. **Verify your service area** (5 mins) — Does it match where you actually work?

4. **Update your details** (5 mins) — Phone, hours, description, website link — are they all current?

5. **Upload fresh photos** (5 mins) — Add 3–5 recent images of your work.

6. **Send one review request** (5 mins) — Message a recent happy customer.

Thirty minutes of focused work can turn a neglected profile into one that gives customers clearer, more up-to-date information.

## What to Expect

Fixing your Google Business Profile won't flood you with leads overnight. That's not how it works.

What it will do:
- Make you easier to find when people search for your services
- Help you [look professional and trustworthy](/blog/tradesmen-website-what-matters)
- Give potential customers reasons to choose you over competitors
- Support a steady flow of enquiries over time

What it won't do:
- Generate instant results
- Guarantee a specific number of jobs
- Replace the quality of your work or your customer service
- Work if you only do it once and forget about it

The businesses that benefit most are consistent. They maintain their profile, post regularly, and treat their online presence as part of their business — not an afterthought.
