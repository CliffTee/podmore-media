# 10 Quick Website Checks Every Tradesperson Should Do This Summer

Summer can be one of the busiest times of year for many trades and local service businesses.

Jobs are booked in, customers want work finished while the weather is good, and there is not much spare time for sitting down to "sort the website".

But that is exactly why a quick website check is useful.

Your website does not need a full rebuild to work better. A few small fixes can make it easier for customers to understand what you do, trust your business and get in touch.

Here are ten practical checks a tradesperson can do in under an hour.

## 1. Is it clear what you actually do?

A visitor should understand your main services within a few seconds.

If your homepage says something vague like "quality solutions for homes and businesses", it is probably not clear enough.

Be specific.

A plumber might mention emergency plumbing, leak repairs, bathroom installations and general maintenance. An electrician might mention fault finding, lighting, consumer-unit work, inspections and small electrical jobs.

You do not need to list every task you have ever done. You do need to make your main services obvious.

A useful check is to open your homepage and ask:

"Would a first-time visitor understand what I do without scrolling far?"

If not, your opening section may need clearer wording.

## 2. Are your contact details easy to find?

A good website should make contacting you simple.

Check that your phone number, email address or enquiry form is easy to find on both desktop and mobile.

Do not hide the contact option at the bottom of a long page if most customers want to ask a quick question or request a quote.

Also test the basics:

* tap the phone number on your mobile;
* submit a test enquiry form;
* check that the email address is correct;
* make sure the form confirmation message makes sense;
* check that enquiries arrive in the right inbox.

Lost enquiries often come from small technical problems rather than a lack of interest.

## 3. Does the site work well on a mobile phone?

Many local customers will visit your site from a mobile phone.

They may be standing in a kitchen with a leak, parked outside a property, or quickly comparing businesses during a lunch break.

Open your website on your own phone and check:

* the text is readable without zooming;
* the buttons are easy to tap;
* the menu is simple to use;
* the page does not jump around while loading;
* images fit the screen;
* contact options work properly.

If the mobile version is awkward, some customers will simply choose another business.

## 4. Does it load quickly enough?

A slow website can frustrate visitors before they have read a word.

You do not need to become a technical expert, but you should check whether the site feels slow on a normal phone connection.

Common causes include:

* oversized images;
* unnecessary scripts;
* old plugins;
* cheap or overloaded hosting;
* heavy animations;
* videos loading automatically.

Tools such as Google Lighthouse or PageSpeed Insights can help identify problems, but the first test is simple: does the site feel quick enough for a real customer?

If a page takes too long to load, speak to whoever manages your website.

## 5. Is your information up to date?

Outdated information makes a business look neglected.

Check:

* services;
* service areas;
* phone number;
* email address;
* opening hours;
* team details;
* prices, if shown;
* guarantees, if mentioned;
* offers or seasonal notices;
* qualification or accreditation references.

Remove old promotions, expired dates and anything that no longer reflects how the business operates.

A customer should not have to wonder whether the website has been abandoned.

## 6. Do your website and Google Business Profile match?

Your website and Google Business Profile should tell the same basic story.

Check that your business name, phone number, website, hours, service areas and main services are consistent.

If your website says one thing and your Google Business Profile says another, customers may become unsure. Search systems may also have less clear information to work with.

For a deeper check, read the Podmore Media guide to [improving an underperforming Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-not-working).

## 7. Are there real trust signals?

Customers want to know whether they can trust you.

Your website can help by showing genuine evidence, such as:

* real photos of your work;
* a short explanation of your experience;
* qualifications or trade memberships where relevant;
* customer reviews or testimonials;
* examples of the types of jobs you handle;
* clear information about the area you cover;
* a simple explanation of what happens after someone contacts you.

Avoid fake reviews, stock-photo teams and exaggerated claims.

Trust is built by making the business feel real, clear and contactable. We have also covered [how a website helps tradespeople build trust](/blog/tradesmen-website-what-matters) in more detail.

## 8. Are your calls to action clear?

A call to action simply tells the visitor what to do next.

Examples include:

* Call now;
* Request a quote;
* Send an enquiry;
* Book a visit;
* Ask a question;
* View our services.

Do not leave visitors guessing.

Each important page should have a sensible next step.

If someone has just read about a service, make it easy for them to enquire about that service. If they are reading a helpful blog article, link naturally to a related service, guide or contact section.

## 9. Do your pages answer common customer questions?

Think about the questions customers regularly ask before booking.

For example:

* Do you cover my area?
* Do you do small jobs?
* How soon can you attend?
* Are you insured?
* What information do you need from me?
* Do you work evenings or weekends?
* What happens after I send an enquiry?

Your website does not need a huge FAQ page, but it should answer the questions that matter most.

Helpful content should be written for real people, not only for search engines. A clear answer to a common customer question can save time, support [getting found on Google](/blog/get-found-on-google-tradespeople) and build trust before the first call.

## 10. Is the site secure and backed up?

Security is easy to ignore until something goes wrong.

Check that your site loads with `https://` and shows the padlock symbol in the browser.

You should also know:

* who hosts the site;
* who has admin access;
* whether the site is backed up;
* how often backups are taken;
* who to contact if the site breaks;
* whether old users or agencies still have access.

If you are not sure, ask your website provider.

A simple access and backup check can prevent a lot of stress later.

## A quick website checklist for tradespeople

Here is the short version:

1. Make your main services clear.
2. Test your phone number, email and forms.
3. Check the site on a mobile phone.
4. Look for slow-loading pages.
5. Remove outdated information.
6. Match your website and Google Business Profile.
7. Add genuine trust signals.
8. Make the next step obvious.
9. Answer common customer questions.
10. Check security, access and backups.

These checks will not guarantee more enquiries. No website checklist can do that.

But they can make your business easier to understand, easier to trust and easier to contact.

That is a worthwhile improvement, especially before a busy period.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a tradesperson check their website?

A simple check every few months is sensible. You should also review the site whenever your services, prices, contact details, opening hours or service areas change.

### Do I need a full website rebuild?

Not always. Many trade websites can be improved with clearer wording, better contact options, updated photos, faster pages and more accurate service information.

### Should I show prices on my website?

It depends on the service. Some trades can show fixed prices or starting prices, while others need to quote after seeing the job. If you do show prices, keep them accurate and explain what is included.

### Are customer reviews useful on a trade website?

Yes, if they are genuine and used honestly. Reviews can help potential customers understand how your business communicates, works and solves problems.

### Is mobile speed really important?

Yes. Many local customers use their phones to compare businesses. If your website is slow or awkward on mobile, some visitors may leave before contacting you.

## Conclusion

Your website does not need to be perfect.

It does need to be clear, current and easy to use.

A few practical checks can help customers understand what you do, see why they should trust you and take the next step.
