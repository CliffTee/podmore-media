# From Van to Website: What Actually Matters for Tradesmen Online

*Published: June 2026 | Podmore Media*

---

Your van is a moving advert for your business. Your tools show the quality of your trade. But for many potential customers, your website is the first place they decide whether your business looks credible.

They may want to check the areas you cover, the services you provide, examples of your work and how to contact you. If the website is slow, unclear or looks neglected, they may move on to another business.

The good news is that you do not need an expensive or complicated website. You need a clear, fast and trustworthy one that helps customers understand what you do and what to do next.

This guide covers the website elements that matter most — without unnecessary features, jargon or inflated costs.

## What Your Website Actually Needs

### 1. Clear Identity (Above the Fold)

"Above the fold" means what people see before they scroll. This is prime real estate. Waste it, and visitors bounce.

**What you need:**
- Your business name and logo
- What you do (in plain English, not jargon)
- Where you work (specific towns/areas)
- A phone number that actually works
- One clear action: "Call Now" or "Get a Free Quote"

**What kills conversions:**
- Sliders with multiple messages (nobody watches them)
- Generic stock photos of smiling families
- Paragraphs of text about your company history
- No clear phone number
- "Welcome to our website" (wasted words)

**Example that works:**
*"Emergency Plumbing in Manchester & Salford. Available 24/7. Call 0161 XXX XXXX Now."*

That's it. Everything else is secondary.

### 2. Proof You Exist (and Are Good)

Trust is everything online. Anyone can make a website. Customers need evidence you're legitimate.

**Essential trust signals:**
- Real photos of you (not stock images)
- Photos of your work (before/after if possible)
- Real customer reviews (with names and locations)
- Any certifications or accreditations
- How long you've been in business
- Physical address (even if it's just your town)

**Red flags that send visitors away:**
- No photos of actual work
- Generic stock images
- No reviews or testimonials
- No location information
- No phone number (just a contact form)

The bar is low here. Most trades websites are terrible. A few real photos and genuine reviews put you ahead of 80% of competitors.

### 3. What You Actually Do

List your services clearly. But here's the key: organise them by what customers search for, not by your internal categories.

**Wrong:**
- "Our Services"
- "Domestic Work"
- "Commercial Work"
- "Maintenance Contracts"

**Right:**
- "Emergency Plumbing"
- "Boiler Repairs"
- "Bathroom Installation"
- "Leak Detection"
- "Blocked Drains"

Use the words customers actually use. Not industry terms. Not internal jargon. The words they type into Google when they have a problem.

Each service should have its own brief page or section explaining:
- What the service includes
- Rough pricing (or "from £X")
- How quickly you can respond
- Why they should choose you for this specific service

### 4. Where You Work

Local SEO is crucial for trades. Your website needs to make your service area crystal clear.

**Best practice:**
- List specific towns and postcodes you cover
- Create individual pages for major areas ("Plumber in Bolton", "Plumber in Wigan")
- Mention local landmarks or areas customers recognise
- Include a map (embedded Google Map)

**Why this matters:** Google uses this information to show your website to people searching in those areas. Vague "we cover the North West" doesn't cut it.

### 5. How to Contact You

This sounds obvious, but most trades websites hide their contact information or make it complicated.

**Essential:**
- Phone number at the top of every page (clickable on mobile)
- Phone number repeated throughout the page
- A simple contact form (name, phone, email, message)
- Response time promise ("We reply within 2 hours")
- Alternative contact methods (WhatsApp, email)

**Advanced (but worth it):**
- Live chat (can be AI-powered)
- Online booking calendar
- Emergency contact form (prioritised)

**Conversion killer:** Contact forms with 10 fields asking for information you don't need. Every extra field reduces submissions.

## What You Don't Need

Let's save you time and money. Here are things trades websites often include that don't help:

### Fancy Design

You don't need animations, video backgrounds, or parallax scrolling. Clean, fast, and professional beats fancy every time.

### Blog (Unless You'll Maintain It)

A blog with one post from 2019 looks worse than no blog at all. If you're not going to publish regularly, skip it.

### Social Media Feeds

Embedding your Twitter feed on your website just shows how infrequently you post. Link to your profiles, don't display them.

### E-commerce

Unless you're selling products directly, you don't need a shop. Services are quoted, not added to a basket.

### Multiple Pages of "About Us" Content

One brief page about your business is enough. Customers care more about what you can do for them than your company history.

## The Mobile Reality

Over 70% of local searches happen on mobile phones. Your website must work perfectly on small screens.

**Mobile essentials:**
- Click-to-call phone numbers
- Large, easy-to-tap buttons
- Fast loading (under 3 seconds)
- Readable text without zooming
- Simple navigation (hamburger menu is fine)

**Test it yourself:** Pull out your phone right now. Search for your website. Try to:
- Find your phone number
- Click to call
- See what services you offer
- Fill out your contact form

If any of those are difficult, fix them immediately. You're losing mobile customers every day.

## Speed Matters (More Than You Think)

Google considers page speed a ranking factor. But more importantly, customers don't wait.

**The numbers:**
- 53% of mobile users abandon sites that take over 3 seconds to load
- Every 1-second delay reduces conversions by 7%
- Slow websites feel unprofessional

**What slows trades websites down:**
- Huge, unoptimised images
- Cheap shared hosting
- Too many plugins or scripts
- Unnecessary animations

**Quick wins:**
- Compress images before uploading (use TinyPNG)
- Choose decent hosting (not the cheapest option)
- Don't upload 4MB photos from your phone
- Test speed with Google PageSpeed Insights

## The DIY vs. Professional Decision

Should you build your own website or hire someone?

**DIY makes sense if:**
- You're comfortable with technology
- You have time to learn
- Your budget is tight
- You use simple tools (Wix, Squarespace, or AI website builders)

**Hire a professional if:**
- You value your time over the cost
- You need custom functionality
- You want ongoing support
- Your website is central to your growth strategy

**The middle ground:** Use AI-powered website builders designed for trades. Tools like Durable, B12, or even ChatGPT-assisted WordPress setups can get you 80% of the way there for a fraction of professional costs.

**Budget reality check:**
- DIY with basic tools: £10-30/month
- AI-assisted builder: £30-80/month
- Professional (simple site): £1,500-3,000 one-time
- Professional (with ongoing support): £200-500/month

## SEO Basics for Trades

You don't need to be an SEO expert. But you need to understand the basics.

**On-page SEO (what's on your website):**
- Use your target keywords naturally ("plumber in Manchester")
- Include keywords in page titles and headings
- Write unique content for each page
- Optimise images (descriptive filenames, alt text)
- Make sure your site loads fast

**Local SEO (showing up in your area):**
- Claim and optimise your Google Business Profile
- Get listed in local directories (Yell, Checkatrade, etc.)
- Ensure your name, address, and phone number are consistent everywhere
- Get reviews on Google

**Technical SEO (behind the scenes):**
- Submit your sitemap to Google Search Console
- Make sure your site is secure (HTTPS)
- Fix broken links
- Ensure mobile-friendliness

Most of this is set-and-forget. Do it once, check it quarterly.

## Content That Converts

If you do nothing else, create these essential pages:

**Homepage:** Who you are, what you do, where you work, how to contact you. Keep it simple.

**Services pages:** One page per major service. Include pricing guidance, response times, and why choose you.

**Areas covered:** List every town and postcode you serve. Create individual pages for major locations.

**About page:** Brief background, qualifications, why you started the business. Include real photos.

**Contact page:** Multiple ways to reach you, response time promises, emergency contact options.

**Reviews/Testimonials:** Dedicated page showcasing customer feedback. Update it regularly.

**FAQ page:** Answer common questions. Reduces repetitive enquiries and builds trust.

That's it. Seven pages. Everything else is optional.

## Measuring What Matters

You can't improve what you don't measure. But most trades track vanity metrics instead of what actually matters.

**Don't obsess over:**
- Total website visitors (irrelevant if they don't call)
- Time on site (longer isn't always better)
- Social media followers (doesn't pay the bills)

**Do track:**
- Phone calls from the website (use a tracking number)
- Contact form submissions
- Quote requests
- Conversion rate (enquiries divided by visitors)
- Which pages generate the most enquiries

**Simple tracking setup:**
- Google Analytics (free, shows visitor behaviour)
- Google Search Console (free, shows search performance)
- Call tracking number (£10-30/month, shows call volume)

Check these monthly. Look for trends. Double down on what's working.

## Common Mistakes (And How to Fix Them)

**Mistake #1: No clear call to action**
*Fix:* Add "Call Now" buttons throughout. Make your phone number impossible to miss.

**Mistake #2: Outdated information**
*Fix:* Set a monthly reminder to review and update. Remove old offers, update services, refresh photos.

**Mistake #3: Hiding pricing**
*Fix:* Give ballpark figures or "from £X" pricing. Customers want to know if you're in their budget.

**Mistake #4: Generic content**
*Fix:* Write specifically about your area, your services, your approach. AI can help, but add your personality.

**Mistake #5: Ignoring mobile**
*Fix:* Test everything on your phone. If it's hard for you, it's hard for customers.

**Mistake #6: Set and forget**
*Fix:* Schedule monthly website reviews. Check for broken links, update content, add new reviews.

## The Bottom Line

Your website doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to be clear, fast, and trustworthy. It needs to answer three questions in under 10 seconds:

1. What do you do?
2. Where do you do it?
3. How do I contact you?

Everything else is secondary.

Most tradesmen overthink websites. They delay launching because it's not "ready." They spend thousands on features nobody uses. They focus on design over function.

The tradesmen winning online have simple, fast websites that make it incredibly easy to contact them. That's it. No magic. No secret sauce. Just clarity and convenience.

Your van gets you to the job. Your tools let you do the work. Your website gets you the call in the first place. Treat it as essential equipment, not an optional extra.

Start simple. Launch fast. Improve over time. But start.
